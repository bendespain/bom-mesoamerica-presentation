# Slide-by-Slide Script — Book of Mormon in Mesoamerica

Designed for ~15–20 minutes spoken, or ~10 minutes tight. Each slide: headline + 3–5 bullets + one visual suggestion.

---

## SLIDE 1 — Title
**Headline:** The Book of Mormon in Mesoamerica
**Subhead:** The Pasión River Model — Geography, Wars, and Maya Kings
**Visual:** Satellite/LiDAR view of the Petén jungle with the Pasión River highlighted.
**Say:** "For decades people have asked: where were the lands of the Book of Mormon? This model puts them in the Maya lowlands of Guatemala and Belize — and the newest evidence comes from the final wars."

## SLIDE 2 — The Big Picture
**Headline:** One model. Three lines of evidence.
1. **Geography** — rivers, seas, a narrow neck, a narrow strip of wilderness, all matching real terrain.
2. **Chronology** — Maya events dated by stelae line up with Book of Mormon dates, year by year.
3. **Inscriptions & monuments** — 23+ Maya records (stelae, murals, tombs) tied to specific passages in Mormon.
**Visual:** Venn diagram of the three lines.
**Say:** "No single piece is proof. Together they form a cumulative case."

## SLIDE 3 — The Map
**Headline:** The lands, placed on the real map
- **Land southward (Nephite heartland):** northern Petén, Guatemala
- **River Sidon:** the Pasión River — runs north, moderate size, east of Zarahemla
- **Narrow strip of wilderness:** the Polochic fault line
- **Narrow neck / narrow pass:** the ~14-mile corridor between Tikal and Uaxactun
- **East sea:** Caribbean · **West sea:** Gulf of Mexico · **South sea:** Pacific
- **Land northward (Cumorah):** northern Belize / southern Quintana Roo
**Visual:** Real map of Guatemala/Belize/Mexico with Book of Mormon labels overlaid (BYU's relational map rotated upright matches this model without distortion).
**Say:** "Notice the shape — an upright hourglass, exactly as the text's relational map describes. Most other models have to rotate theirs sideways. This one doesn't."

## SLIDE 4 — Zarahemla = Ceibal
**Headline:** The capital city: Ceibal, Guatemala
**Why Ceibal fits:**
- Population surge at the Mulekite arrival (~587 BC)
- Major influx when Mosiah's Nephites arrived from the land of Nephi
- Ceramics from the Salama Valley (land of Nephi) found at the site
- Construction boom after Christ, then ~200 years of peace (temple building paused)
- Abandoned ~AD 325 — right when Mormon 2 says the Nephites retreated north under the 350 treaty
- 30+ triadic structures — unique in Mesoamerica at this frequency; matches "many churches" (Mosiah 25)
- Two city sectors on LiDAR — possibly Mulekite and Nephite quarters
- A moderate river to the east running north; steep banks; fortified; center of the land
**Visual:** LiDAR image of Ceibal with the Pasión River labeled "Sidon."
**Say:** "Harvard's Gordon Willey excavated Ceibal in the 1960s and documented six phases. They map onto Zarahemla's story almost beat for beat."

## SLIDE 6 — The Four Final Wars (overview)
**Headline:** AD 320 → 385. Four wars. One ending.
1. **~327–350** — The war that produced the treaty
2. **~360** — The epistle war
3. **~375** — The Desolation war
4. **~379–385** — The Entrada: final destruction at Cumorah
**Visual:** Timeline bar with Maya events stacked under Book of Mormon dates.
**Say:** "The report matches these wars to Maya monuments — kings, stelae, tombs. Some ties are circumstantial. The chronology is not."

## SLIDE 7 — War 1: The 350 Treaty
**Headline:** Mormon 2 — the treaty that split the land
- Mormon 2:3 — Lamanites attack in AD 327; Nephites retreat north. Ceibal abandoned ~325.
- Mormon 2:9 — King **Aaron** of the Lamanites, 44,000 men. The only known Maya king at that time: **Foliated Jaguar** of Tikal.
- Mormon 2:28 — treaty in AD 350 dividing lands at the narrow passage. Gadianton robbers involved nine times before, never again after.
- **Caracol** (Te' K'ab Chaak) — the only significant city in the Maya Mountains near Zarahemla, 70 miles from Tikal. Proposed Gadianton base. King dies ~349; Caracol goes archaeologically quiet for years.
**Visual:** Map showing Tikal, Caracol, Ceibal triangle.
**Say:** "The robbers vanish from the record the moment the treaty lands. So does Caracol's king."

---

## SLIDE 8 — War 2: The 360 Epistle
**Headline:** Mormon 3:4 — a letter from a king
- "In the 360th year, the king of the Lamanites sent an epistle unto me."
- **Chak Tok Ich'aak** (Great Jaguar Paw) becomes ruler of Tikal that same year — recorded on Stela 31.
- Four stelae confirm he was in the area during the 360 war.
- Lamanites lack the strength to finish the job; eight years of peace follow.
**Visual:** Stela 31 photo.
**Say:** "One king. One letter. Same year."

---

## SLIDE 9 — War 3: The 375 Assault
**Headline:** Mormon 4 — Desolation falls
- Mormon 4:16–19 — Lamanites come "with all their powers"; attack the city of Desolation.
- Desolation = **Uaxactun**, ~20 miles north of Tikal, near the narrow neck.
- Where did the extra manpower come from? The text doesn't say. The archaeology does: **Teotihuacan**.
- Spearthrower Owl, Teotihuacan's ruler, invades Tikal and kills Great Jaguar Paw.
- The ~14-mile Tikal–Uaxactun earthwork matches Alma's "fortification on the line" (Alma 50:34; Helaman 4:7).
**Visual:** The Tikal–Uaxactun fortification line on a map.
**Say:** "Maya forces alone may not have been enough. Someone from the outside entered the picture."

---

## SLIDE 10 — War 4: The Entrada
**Headline:** AD 378–385 — the most important event in Maya history
- **Siyaj K'ahk'** ("Fire Is Born"), general of Spearthrower Owl, arrives in the Tikal region in AD 378. Recorded at La Sufricaya — east of Tikal, near the proposed western boundary of Cumorah.
- Mormon 5:5: "thus three hundred and seventy and nine years passed away" — one year after the Entrada. Time to organize a five-year campaign.
- After Uaxactun, Fire Is Born recruits warriors at Naachtun, Rio Azul, Holmul — all east of the proposed Cumorah boundary.
- Mormon 6: gathering at Cumorah. 22 commanders, 10,000 each = 220,000 Nephite warriors. Lamanite numbers estimated 400,000–500,000.
- Population math: 50,000 survivors of Christ's coming, growing 1.5%/year for 350 years ≈ **5 million** Nephites.
**Visual:** The La Sufricaya mural; timeline AD 378 → 379 → 385.
**Say:** "The Maya call it the Entrada. Mormon calls it the end."

---

## SLIDE 11 — The Cumulative Case
**Headline:** Why this holds together
1. **Chronology:** AD 327, 350, 360, 375, 378, 379, 385 — Maya dates and Book of Mormon dates, matched.
2. **Geography:** upright hourglass, north-running Sidon, narrow neck between two seas, fortified line.
3. **Texts:** 30+ passages in Mormon 1–6 compared to monuments; glyph panels reading "it came to pass"; triadic structures and "many churches."
4. **Scale:** a war of hundreds of thousands fits a region that held millions.
**Visual:** Summary graphic of all three lines with the 23 monuments counted.
**Say:** "Each thread alone is suggestive. Woven together, they're hard to dismiss."

---

## SLIDE 12 — Honest Caveats
**Headline:** What this is — and isn't
- This is a **proposed correlation**, built by Richard Terry and Robert Roylance (Pasión River Model).
- Mainstream Mesoamerican archaeology does not accept Book of Mormon correlations.
- Some king-identifications (Aaron = Foliated Jaguar, etc.) are interpretive.
- The strength is convergence: many independent lines pointing the same direction.
**Visual:** Simple two-column: "What the evidence shows" vs. "What it doesn't prove."
**Say:** "Present it as a case, not a conclusion. That's what makes it credible."
