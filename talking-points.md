# Talking Points & FAQ

## Quick answers

**Q: Isn't the traditional view that Cumorah is in New York?**
A: That's the popular tradition, but the text itself never says the New York hill is the Book of Mormon Cumorah. This model puts the final battle at Hill Achotal in northern Belize and treats the New York hill as where Moroni later deposited the plates after years of wandering.

**Q: What about horses, steel, and other anachronisms?**
A: Outside this document's scope — but Mesoamerican models generally handle them via translation semantics ("horse" = tapir in some readings) or limited Old World introductions. See Sorenson's *An Ancient American Setting*.

**Q: How do you respond to "no archaeological proof of the Nephites"?**
A: Reframe: the model doesn't ask archaeology to find "Nephites" as a labeled culture. It asks whether Book of Mormon events, dates, and geography converge with what Maya archaeology already documents. The Entrada of 378 is real, dated, and recorded. The 350 treaty's abandonment signature is real. The convergence is the argument.

**Q: Isn't matching kings to Book of Mormon figures just pattern-matching?**
A: Some of it is. The report itself flags circumstantial ties. The non-circumstantial core is chronological: Maya events dated independently by stelae land on Book of Mormon years. That's harder to wave off.

**Q: Why Mesoamerica and not the Heartland (North America)?**
A: The text requires two seas east and west of a narrow neck, a river running north, and a land southward with a wilderness strip. Mesoamerica has all of it in one compact region. North American models struggle with the seas and the river.

**Q: What's the single strongest piece of evidence?**
A: Probably Ceibal. Six occupational phases documented by Harvard, mapping onto Zarahemla's story from Mulekite arrival through the 350 abandonment — discovered by excavators with no Book of Mormon agenda.

## Phrases that land well
- "No single piece is proof. Together they form a cumulative case."
- "The Maya call it the Entrada. Mormon calls it the end."
- "Present it as a case, not a conclusion."
- "If the geography is real, the history might be too."

## Phrases to avoid
- "This proves the Book of Mormon." → say "this is consistent with"
- "Archaeology confirms." → say "archaeology doesn't contradict, and in places it aligns"
- Absolute certainty about any one king identification.
